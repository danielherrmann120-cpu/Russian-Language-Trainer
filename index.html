Советы — Learning Tips

Practical guidance for beginners.

Pronunciation Basics

  • Russian vowels shift when unstressed — "О" sounds like "a" when not stressed.
  • Stress is unpredictable — must be memorized per word.
  • Р is always rolled (trilled), like Spanish R.
  • Ж sounds like the "s" in measure — zh.
  • Х is a guttural "kh" like Scottish loch.
  • Ы has no English equivalent — say "i" but with the back of your tongue raised.

Grammar Essentials

  • Russian has no articles — no "a" or "the".
  • Word order is flexible due to a case system (6 cases).
  • Verbs have two aspects: imperfective (ongoing) and perfective (completed).
  • Nouns have grammatical gender: masculine, feminine, neuter.
  • Adjectives agree with the noun they describe in gender, number, and case.

Alphabet Learning Tips

  • ~10 letters look and sound like English: А, Е, З, К, М, О, Т.
  • Watch out for false friends: В = V, Н = N, Р = R, С = S, У = OO, Х = KH.
  • Learn to read first, even before memorizing vocabulary.
  • Write each letter by hand — muscle memory speeds up recognition.

Useful Resources

  • Practice the Russian keyboard layout early on.
  • Watch Russian media with subtitles to train your ear.
  • Apps like Duolingo and Anki pair well with this app.
  • Speaking out loud — even alone — dramatically improves retention.
  • Learn 100 most common words first; they cover ~50% of everyday speech.

Polite Registers

  • Russian has two "you": ты (ty) — informal, and вы (vy) — formal/plural.
  • Use вы with strangers, elders, and in professional settings.
  • Use ты with friends, family, and children.
  • Switching from вы to ты signals growing familiarity.

Common Patterns

  • Many Russian nouns ending in -ция match English -tion words (станция = station).
  • Words ending in -ист often match English -ist (турист = tourist).
  • -ский / -ская endings mean "-ish" or "-ic" (русский = Russian).
  • Negation is simple: put не before the verb (я не знаю = I don't know).
